Your own maps and handy tools on the geocaching.com maps

This script turns the geocaching.com maps into a flexible map workbench. Choose Kartverket topographic maps, OpenStreetMap, aerial photos or any map service you add yourself, and get place search, coordinates in every common format, measuring, waypoint projection and 161 m circles around traditional caches and caches with solved coordinates – all in one tidy toolbar at the right edge of the map.

Works on www.geocaching.com/map and www.geocaching.com/play/map. Your chosen map is also shown on the small map on cache pages. The interface is available in English and Norwegian.

New: routes for your GPS

Plan a caching trip on the map and take it with you. Click the Route button in the toolbar:

  • Pick geocaches and pins from a searchable list – nearby caches by name or code, any GC code worldwide, places and addresses – or simply click them on the map.
  • Put the stops in order (or sort by nearest) and see the distance and bearing of every leg – or tick Back to the start for a round trip.
  • Preview the whole route on the map and exactly what goes into the file, then download it as a GPX route for Garmin and most other GPS units – with the Geocache, Geocache Found and blue pin symbols.
  • Tick Full cache data to get every geocache in the file as a full paperless cache – description, hint, attributes, logs and additional waypoints, in the same format as the GPX file on the cache page.

New: the geocaches on the map in a list

On /map the Geocaches on the map button opens a list of the caches on the map you see, nearest the middle first – with Solved, Found and Owned tags, D/T and favourite points. It follows the map as you move it. Point at a cache on the map to highlight it in the list; click a line to zoom all the way in, with a soft glow on the cache, and click again to go back. A button on each line opens the cache page in a new tab. The list and the route window sit at the top right or top left of the map, clear of the side panel.

Features

Maps and overlays

  • Built-in background maps: Kartverket Topo, Kartverket Gråtone, Kartverket Toporaster, OpenTopoMap, Esri World Imagery, OpenStreetMap and Esri World Topo – or keep the geocaching.com map.
  • Overlays: Kartverket hiking and ski routes, Kartverket property boundaries and Esri hillshade – as many as you like at once.
  • Dimming towards white: one slider fades the background map (by at most 50 %) so the caches stand out; another sets the opacity of the overlays (50–100 %). The geocaching.com map is hidden while your own background is selected, so it never shows through.
  • Cache icons always on top of your maps.
  • The layer panel has three tabs – geocaching.com's own maps, background maps and overlays – so the lists stay short.
  • Drag maps up and down to set your own order; the overlay at the top of the list is drawn on top.
  • Remove maps you never use – including geocaching.com's own – and bring them back whenever you like.

Add your own map services

Paste almost any map link and press Detect. The layer editor understands:

  • XYZ / TMS tile addresses with {z}, {x} and {y} (also {-y}, {s} and quadkeys)
  • a single map tile copied from the browser's developer tools
  • WMTS and WMS services – the layer list is fetched for you
  • ArcGIS addresses ending in /MapServer

Preview the layer on the map before saving, and import or export your layers as a file to move them to another computer or share them.

Find a place

  • One combined list as you type: Norwegian place names and addresses (Kartverket), places abroad (OpenStreetMap via Photon) coordinates and GC codes (posted and solved coordinates).
  • Press / on the map to open the search, ↑ ↓ and Enter to pick a result.
  • A home button takes you to your saved home address with one click.

Coordinates

  • The coordinates under the mouse are shown at the bottom right – click to switch format. The zoom level is shown above them: type a number to zoom. Hide the readout with its ✕, and show it again in Settings.
  • Right-click the map for a popup with the position (always opens in DMM). Click the line to switch format, and copy with one click. Google Street View opens Street View at the point.
  • Snaps to geocaches: right-click on or next to a cache and the popup uses the cache's coordinates and shows its GC code and name. Click the GC code to open the cache page in a new tab. The popup also shows the cache's favourite points (♥). On /play/map our popup replaces the site's right-click menu (configurable). Right-clicking one of your pins snaps to it too.
FormatExample
DMMN 59° 54.834 E 010° 45.132
DD59.913900, 10.752200
DMSN 59° 54' 50.0" E 10° 45' 07.9"
UTM (automatic zone)32V 597980E 6643119N
UTM 32 / 33 / 35 (fixed zone)33V 262560E 6649444N

UTM is computed with the Krüger series, accurate to about a millimetre.

Measuring, projection and 161 m circles

  • Measure distance and bearing by clicking points on the map.
  • Project a waypoint from any point: type bearing and distance (m, km or ft) and see the result on the map as you type.
  • 161 m circles wherever something is placed (from zoom level 15): traditional caches at their exact coordinates, caches with solved (corrected) coordinates at the solved position, visible physical stages and final locations of multis, mysteries, Wherigos and letterboxes (read from the cache page). Where no cache icon shows the spot, a clickable mark opens the cache page – a smiley, like the site's own, for solved caches you have found. The marks at solved caches can also be shown without circles (own toolbar button). Hovering a solved cache shows Final location – also your own and unfound ones, where the site's icon and popup are kept; solved caches can get a small type icon, since the site's “solved” icon doesn't show the type (Settings). Solved caches are remembered, so their mark shows even when the posted coordinates are far off the map (Wherigos, puzzles). Or draw a single circle anywhere.
  • Hide all geocaches (and their 161 m circles) with one click for a clean map, and show them again.
  • Favourite points: a red ♥ and the number above caches on the map from zoom 15 – only for caches the site shows, from a minimum you choose in Settings. Turn them on and off with the heart button in the toolbar.
  • Difficulty/terrain (D/T) below every cache on the map from zoom 15, e.g. 1.5/2 – with its own toolbar button and setting.
  • First to find: a red FTF badge on caches nobody has found yet – spot open FTFs at a glance (can be turned off in Settings).
  • Follows the site's filters: caches you leave out in the sidebar on /map (My Finds, My Hides, cache types) lose their circles, marks and favourite points too.
  • Named pins you can rename and export as GPX for your GPS – one pin or all of them; my location, fullscreen, and one button to clear all drawings.

One toolbar

The site's own zoom, location and map-style buttons are folded into the same toolbar, so there are no duplicate buttons. All icons are simple line icons, and all dialogs share one clean style.

  • Put the toolbar on the right (default) or left, or centred at the top or bottom – in one or two rows (two by itself when one doesn't fit). Drag it by its grip to another edge, or choose in Settings.
  • It keeps clear of the site's Search for geocaches panel and follows it as it opens and closes.
  • Settings (gear button): toolbar position, language, starting with the site's side panel folded away (to see as much map as possible), and whether to show geocaching.com's own location and map-style buttons (its zoom buttons are always hidden).
  • The toolbar is centred along its edge and keeps clear of other controls.
  • The layer panel and all dialogs open beside the toolbar, never on top of it or other controls, and can be dragged anywhere; the layer panel, search and settings open where you left them.

Privacy

The script collects and sends no personal data (to recognise your own caches it compares their owner name with your user name, in your browser only) and has no server of its own. It only contacts the map servers of the layers you switch on, Kartverket (ws.geonorge.no) and Photon (photon.komoot.io) when you search, a map service's capabilities address when you add a WMS/WMTS layer, geocaching.com's own cache search for the area in view (when the 161 m circles, the marks or the favourite points are on), and – with the circles on – the cache pages of multis, mysteries, Wherigos and letterboxes in view (for their visible stages), and – from zoom 15 or when you right-click – the pages of caches the search left out (typically your own; at most 20 per view, 60 with My Hides off, once per visit) and the page of a GC code you search for, to read their exact coordinates. Your location is only requested when you click the location button, and Google Street View only opens when you click it. All settings are stored in your browser. The script needs no special permissions (@grant none).

Good to know

  • Only map services in Web Mercator (EPSG:3857) can be shown, because the geocaching.com map uses that projection.
  • Right-clicking a cache gives its exact coordinates – your corrected ones for solved caches – once geocaching.com's cache search has answered for the area. Caches the search leaves out, such as your own, are read from their cache page – in the background from zoom 15, or on the first right-click. Physical stages whose coordinates the owner has hidden cannot be found.
  • Something not working after a site update? Open the browser console (F12) on the map page and type gcctReport(). A short report without personal data is copied to the clipboard – please include it in your feedback. For problems with the 161 m circles, gcctCacheDebug() shows what the cache search returned (field names and counts only), gcctStageTest('GC…') shows the stages found on one cache page, and gcctSnapTest('GC…') shows what the script knows about one cache when right-click doesn't snap.
